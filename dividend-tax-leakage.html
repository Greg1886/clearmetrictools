How this calculator works

What it compares

The same ETF in two versions. An accumulating ETF reinvests its dividends inside the fund. A distributing ETF pays them out: you pay tax on them each year, and the calculator reinvests what is left.

How the numbers are calculated

  • Both versions earn the same total return, compounded monthly.
  • For the distributing version, the dividend part (the yield) is paid out every quarter. Tax at the selected rate comes off, and the rest is reinvested.
  • At the end, both are sold, and the gain (final value minus everything paid in, including reinvested dividends) is taxed at the same rate.
  • The accumulating version comes out ahead because the money that would go to dividend tax keeps growing until you sell.

Worked example

Start with €15,000, add €600 a month for 25 years, with a 9% total return of which 1.8% is dividends, taxed at 31.4% (France):

  • Accumulating: €622,957 after tax on the final gain.
  • Distributing: €594,988 after tax, having paid €37,036 in dividend tax along the way.

The distributing version leaves you €27,969 less.

About the country presets

Each preset fills in one rate, which the calculator uses for both dividends and the final gain. Real rules are often more detailed:

  • France: dividends and gains are taxed at 12.8% income tax plus 18.6% social contributions in 2026, 31.4% in total, or on the progressive scale if you choose.
  • Germany: 25% plus the 5.5% solidarity surcharge, 26.375% in total. Not included: the €1,000 yearly allowance (Sparer-Pauschbetrag) and the 30% partial exemption for equity funds (Teilfreistellung).
  • Spain: 19% is the first step of the savings tax scale, which rises to 21%, 23%, 27% and 30%.
  • Portugal: 28%, or you can choose to add the income to your other income.
  • Denmark: share income is taxed at 27% up to 79,400 kr in 2026 and 42% above.
  • Sweden: 30% on capital income in a regular account. Many investors use an ISK account instead, taxed each year on a standard deemed income.
  • Norway: 37.84% is 22% multiplied by the 1.72 adjustment factor that applied in 2025.
  • Italy: 26% on capital gains.
  • Netherlands: private investments are taxed in Box 3 on a deemed return (36% in 2026), not on actual dividends and gains, so this comparison doesn't reflect Dutch rules.
  • Switzerland: the 35% withholding tax can be reclaimed by residents who declare the income, so it is not the final tax.
  • For Belgium, Austria, Ireland and Greece, check the current rules with your tax authority.

What it leaves out

  • Foreign withholding tax on dividends inside the fund, which affects both versions.
  • Some countries tax accumulating funds before you sell, which shrinks their advantage.
  • Fund fees, yearly allowances, and changes in the dividend yield over time.
