How this calculator works

What it calculates

How much reaches your bank account when you sell crypto: the sale value, minus the exchange's fee, minus an estimate of the tax on your gain.

  • Exchange fee = sale value × fee %.
  • Gain = sale value − what you paid (cost basis) − exchange fee.
  • Tax = the gain taxed under the country's rules below. With no gain, there is no tax.
  • Money in your bank = sale value − exchange fee − tax.

The rules applied, by country (2026)

  • United States: crypto is treated as property. Gains on crypto held more than one year are long-term, and gains held one year or less are short-term, taxed as ordinary income. Long-term rates are 0%, 15% or 20% depending on income. The calculator uses an estimated 15% (long-term) or 24% (short-term).
  • United Kingdom: gains above the £3,000 yearly tax-free allowance are taxed at 18% or 24%, depending on your income tax band. The calculator assumes 24%, the higher rate.
  • Germany: crypto held privately for more than one year can be sold tax-free. For shorter holdings, gains are tax-free if your total gains from private sales in the year are below €1,000. At €1,000 or more, the whole gain is taxed at your personal income tax rate, which the calculator estimates at 35%.
  • France: if your total crypto sales in the year are €305 or less, the gains are exempt. Above that, gains are taxed at 12.8% income tax plus social contributions (18.6% in 2026), 31.4% in total. You can opt for the progressive income tax scale instead.
  • Italy: gains realised from 1 January 2026 are taxed at a flat 33%. Euro-denominated e-money tokens (euro stablecoins) are taxed at 26%, which the calculator doesn't separate out.
  • Spain: crypto gains are savings income, taxed on a scale: 19% up to €6,000, 21% up to €50,000, 23% up to €200,000, 27% up to €300,000 and 30% above.
  • Portugal: gains on crypto held 365 days or more are excluded from tax. Shorter holdings are taxed at 28%, or you can choose to add them to your other income.

Worked example

You bought crypto for €2,000 and sell it for €10,000, with a 1.5% exchange fee. The fee is €150, so the gain is €10,000 − €2,000 − €150 = €7,850.

  • France (sales above €305): 31.4% × €7,850 = €2,465 tax, so €7,385 reaches your bank.
  • Portugal, held less than 365 days: 28% × €7,850 = €2,198 tax, so €7,652 reaches your bank.
  • Germany, held more than one year: no tax, so €9,850 reaches your bank.

What it leaves out

  • It treats your sale as one sale of a holding bought for the cost you enter. When you sell only part of your crypto, countries work out the cost differently. France uses a portfolio formula: gain = sale price − (total purchase price × sale price ÷ value of your whole portfolio). Spain uses first in, first out (FIFO).
  • Yearly thresholds, such as France's €305 and Germany's €1,000, apply to all your sales in the year, not one sale.
  • Your other income, losses from other sales, US state taxes and the US net investment income tax, and the special rules for swaps between cryptocurrencies, staking or mining.
