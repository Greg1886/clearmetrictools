How this planner works

What it does

It splits your monthly net income into three parts: 75% for living costs, 15% for investing and 10% for a safety net (emergency savings). It then estimates how much you would need invested to live off your portfolio (financial independence, or FIRE), and how long investing 15% would take to get there.

How the numbers are calculated

  • Target wealth (Auto mode): your yearly lifestyle spending (75% of income × 12) divided by the safe withdrawal rate. At 4%, that is 25 times your yearly spending. In Custom mode, you set the target yourself.
  • Passive income: the target × the withdrawal rate ÷ 12, the monthly amount the portfolio could pay out.
  • Time until independence: the number of months for your existing portfolio plus the monthly 15% to reach the target, growing each month by the expected return ÷ 12.

Worked example

With €2,500 a month, a 4% withdrawal rate, a 9% return and no existing portfolio:

  • Lifestyle €1,875, investing €375 and safety net €250 a month.
  • Target: €1,875 × 12 ÷ 4% = €562,500, which pays out €1,875 a month.
  • Investing €375 a month at 9% reaches it in about 27.9 years.

About the withdrawal rate

A 4% withdrawal rate is a common rule of thumb based on studies of historical US stock and bond returns. It is not a guarantee. A lower rate gives a larger, more cautious target.

What it leaves out

  • Inflation. Your spending and the target stay in today's money, so for a realistic timeline, enter a return after inflation.
  • Taxes, investment fees, pay rises and changes in your spending.
  • Market ups and downs: it assumes the same return every month.
  • The 10% safety net is set aside, not invested.
