How this calculator works

What it compares

Two ways to invest the same money in ETFs for the same number of years:

  • PEA first: your deposits go into a PEA until you have paid in €150,000, the legal cap. Anything you deposit after that goes into a CTO (a standard brokerage account).
  • CTO only: everything goes into a CTO.

The result shows how much more, or less, you keep after tax by using the PEA first.

The tax rules used (2026)

  • PEA held 5 years or more: gains are exempt from income tax. You pay only social contributions of 18.6%.
  • PEA withdrawn before 5 years: a withdrawal generally closes the plan, and the gain is taxed at 12.8% income tax plus 18.6% social contributions, 31.4% in total.
  • CTO: gains are taxed at the flat tax (prélèvement forfaitaire unique) of 31.4%: 12.8% income tax plus 18.6% social contributions.

How the numbers are calculated

  • Each month your deposit is added, and the balance grows by the gross return minus the ETF's yearly fee (TER), divided by 12.
  • Tax is charged once, at the end, on the gain: the final value minus everything you paid in.
  • The €150,000 cap counts what you pay in, not what the account is worth. The PEA can grow well past €150,000.

Worked example

Say each account has made a €10,000 gain, held for more than 5 years.

  • PEA: 18.6% × €10,000 = €1,860 tax, so you keep €8,160.
  • CTO: 31.4% × €10,000 = €3,140 tax, so you keep €6,860.

The PEA leaves you €1,280 more, and the gap grows with the size of the gain. If you start with €15,000 and add €800 a month, your deposits reach the €150,000 cap in month 169, about 14 years in. From then on, new money goes into the CTO.

What it leaves out

  • It assumes accumulating ETFs and no sales until the end. In a real CTO, dividends and sales are taxed in the year they happen, which usually makes the CTO result lower than shown.
  • It uses the flat tax, not the optional progressive income tax scale, which can cost less on a low income.
  • Gains made before 2018 in older PEAs can follow different social contribution rules.
  • It ignores the PEA-PME, broker fees, inflation and future changes to tax law.
